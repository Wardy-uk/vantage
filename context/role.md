## What the job is (Head of Service Delivery, formal JD)

Head of Service Delivery for four brands within nurtur.tech: **BriefYourMarket,
Yomdel, KnowYourMarket, LeadPro**. Described as "champion of client operations,
guardian of production environments and a key voice of the customer into the
business" — technical, but the primary focus is the customer's experience of
**Customer Care, Support, Integrations and Digital Design**.

Standing responsibilities, condensed from the JD:

- Maximise availability of production services — monitoring, incident
  management, service improvement; contracts as a **minimum**.
- Create and manage a team structure that adapts to business and customer need.
- Build customer-centric processes **with a means to monitor and adapt**.
- Motivate the team; manage poor performance; reward outstanding performance.
- "Fly the flag" — showcase positive change and customer feedback to the wider
  business.
- Analyse ticket quality against a "resolve first time" mantra.
- Train the **wider business** on requirement gathering.
- Build a training matrix that nurtures career growth.
- **Use tooling available to create reporting and analyse areas of risk.**
- Play an active role in the **Leadership Team**, delivering projects to agreed
  deadlines.
- Respond to customer escalations with proactive periodic stakeholder updates.
- Meet all KPIs set.

> ⚠ Note the date. "Use tooling available to create reporting and analyse areas
> of risk which would hinder the business from meeting its goals" is in the
> formal JD from **April 2026** — four months before PIP competency 2 asked for
> the same thing. The PIP is not adding a duty; it is enforcing one.

## What he is judged against (HoTS Assignment Framework)

Reports to **Technical Director / CTO**. Direct responsibility for **1st Line

### The six outcomes

1. **Support visibility & business intelligence** — baseline metrics dashboard;
   metrics in business-friendly terms; weekly support summaries to the Technical
   Director and SLT; monthly trend analysis of top ticket drivers; *"demonstrated
   ability to identify trends and proactively escalate systemic issues before
   they become customer-impacting"*.
2. **Tiered support model & escalation excellence** — documented 1st/2nd Line
   boundary and escalation criteria; 1st Line empowered to resolve non-technical
   work; structured escalation to Engineering via the SDM.
3. **Customer satisfaction & service quality** — CSAT, first-response SLA, a QA
   framework with monthly scoring, first-contact resolution, proactive comms on
   known issues. Names **adoption of AI agents** to improve team effectiveness
   and customer sentiment.
4. **Team engagement & development** — 1:1 performance reviews for **100% of
   support staff**; performance rubrics and career progression plans; flight
   risks identified and mitigated; bottom 10% on documented PIPs.
5. **Cross-functional collaboration & engineering alignment** — joint bug triage
   with the SDM; weekly engineering escalation summaries; support insight into
   the product roadmap; 100% attendance at Operations, Delivery, Product and
   Engineering alignment meetings.
6. **Production team performance & quality** — request intake, prioritisation
   and SLAs for templates and print; cross-client rendering QA; capacity
   planning; a shift toward customer self-service.

### The KPI set

First response time (SLA compliance ≥95% both tiers) · resolution time (−10–20%)
· first-contact resolution (+10–20%) · escalation accuracy (≥90% routed right
first time) · 1st Line resolution rate (+15–25%) · CSAT/NPS (+10–15%) · backlog

(−30%) · performance management coverage (100%) · support-related revenue loss
(−50%) · team satisfaction · Production SLA compliance (≥95%) · template error

### The checkpoints are not used

The framework stages its outcomes across Day 15 / 30 / 45 / 60 / 90, and
**carries no start date** — the signature block is blank and no commencement
date is stated. The SFIA workbook records the assignment as "originally a
probationary period, now treated as permanent per Nick."

**Nick's instruction, 3 September 2026: ignore the dates. Everything is to be
demonstrable as of now.**

So VANTAGE discards the staging entirely and treats all six outcomes and all
fourteen KPIs as **standing expectations, live today**. This is the safe reading
as well as the instructed one — the alternative was anchoring day-numbers to a
date nobody has stated, which would have put confident false deadlines into a
tool whose entire purpose is an accurate paper trail.

Two consequences, and they cut in opposite directions:

- **"Not due yet" is not available as a defence.** There is no phase in which an
  outcome has not started.
- **"Not due yet" is equally not available as a reason to defer.** A tool that
  let him park an outcome behind a checkpoint that has no date would be
  manufacturing permission out of a blank field.

The day-numbers are never rendered, and no date is ever computed from them.

## Proposing SFIA grades over time (NOT BUILT)

The intended use is that **VANTAGE proposes Nick's grades from accumulated
evidence**, rather than anyone assigning them up front. Nothing implements this
yet. Constraints for whoever builds it — all of them the house rules, applied to
a new surface:

- **A skill with no evidence renders as "not yet evidenced", never as a low
  grade.** This is the absent-is-not-zero rule in its most dangerous form: a
  fabricated Level 2 against a skill VANTAGE simply cannot see would be a false
  negative about a man's competence, in a document that could reach his manager.
- **Every proposed grade carries its evidence and its date**, the way findings
  do. A grade with no working is an opinion wearing a number.
- **Proposed, never assigned.** VANTAGE has no authority to grade anyone. The
  output is a draft for Nick to accept, reject or take into a conversation.
- **Private by default, and Nick moves it himself.** A proposed grade is a
  judgement about Nick, so it lives on the private side; its obvious use is a
  development conversation with Chris. Nick's decision, 3 Sep 2026: **both are
  true**, and the existing privacy rule already resolves it — private things go
  outward only when he moves them. Nothing outward-facing reads a proposed
  grade; he takes one into a conversation if and when he wants to. No new rule,
  and no exception to the existing one.
- **The team's rows are a separate question.** The matrix grades six other roles.
  Grading his *people* is a different product with different consent problems,

## ⚠ Ticket counts are verification inputs, never performance scores

NOVA's per-person set includes `solvedToday`, `solvedWeek` and `ticketsPerHour`.
Read PIP competency 1 before putting any of them on a card:

> "Overtime was approved based on **headline productivity indicators (ticket
> counts, activity status)** without cross-checking against logged work in
> Jira/Nova."

Those are the same numbers. Building a card that ranks agents by tickets solved
would rebuild, inside the tool meant to evidence the PIP, the exact reasoning
the PIP was opened over.

And yet the five-step checklist Nick must apply to every overtime claim
*requires* them — step one is "verify work against Jira/Nova systems activity".

So the same figures have two roles and only one is legitimate:

- **Verification input — YES.** Shown against a specific overtime claim, to
  answer "did this person do the work they are claiming for".
- **Performance judgement — NO.** Never a ranking, never a RAG, never the basis
  of a card saying somebody is underperforming.

Judgement about quality comes from **QA scores and Golden Rules**, which are
scored assessments of work rather than counts of it. That distinction is the
whole of competency 1 and must be structural here, not a comment.
