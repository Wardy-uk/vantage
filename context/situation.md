# The situation

_As at 18 August 2026. Update the dated facts; the framing has been stable._

## One paragraph

Nick Ward is Head of Service Delivery at Nurtur Limited. The Service Desk is
underperforming for reasons that are mostly structural, but Nick is on a PIP
whose four competencies are all about **evidence and cadence** rather than
strategy. A Support Review commissioned by the Head of Change (w/c 3 Aug 2026)
documented the department's problems in detail. The fact that Nick did not
produce that review himself is the specific thing being held against him.

## The actual test being applied

Ricky's stated position, in his own framing:

- The department "had been decimated" before Chris took over in July 2025 —
  consistently 250+ tickets over SLA.
- Chris's transformation is credited with hiring, promoting, exiting people and
  integrating wider product sets. The department was given back to Nick in
  March 2026.
- Much of the Support Review reads to Ricky as **regression against what Chris
  fixed** — training, knowledge transfer, support from Development.
- His summary: _"Grouped up, a lot of the report points to one root cause:
  leadership."_
- His concern about the PIP: _"if we run your 14–30 day plan while he's on a
  PIP, success tells us very little about his own ability to lead — given it
  wasn't him that surfaced this information, when he should have. We already
  know he delivers when under scrutiny and told what to fix, but that isn't the
  attribute the role needs. So what happens at day 90 when you step back, as
  Chris did?"_

**This is the bar.** Not "did Nick complete the PIP actions" but "did Nick
demonstrate self-directed proactive leadership that survives the removal of
scrutiny."

## What follows from that

Three design consequences for everything in this workspace:

1. **Self-initiated beats correct.** A finding Nick raises before anyone asks is
   worth more than the same finding raised when prompted. Timestamps matter, so
   everything is dated at the point of discovery.
2. **It has to run without him being watched.** A system that only works while
   someone is checking is precisely the failure mode Ricky is predicting. The
   answer to "what happens at day 90" is a mechanism, not a promise.
3. **Owning the fix beats defending the diagnosis.** The Support Review's
   findings are largely fair and largely structural. Arguing about attribution
   loses. Being visibly the person delivering the improvement plan wins.

## The honest counter-argument, for Nick's own use

Not for sending. For keeping perspective when the PIP framing makes everything
feel like a personal failure:

- The review's ten themes are overwhelmingly structural: product estate grew,
  experienced Tier 2/Tier 3/TPJ capability was lost, six fragmented Jira spaces,
  no release-readiness gate, ~200 unprioritised dev tickets, no formal customer
  SLAs, first-line merged into a non-technical Customer Care hiring profile.
- Exactly **one** of the ten themes (theme 6, management cadence and development
  conversations) lands squarely within Nick's control.
- Several named causes — Development responsiveness, project governance, pay and
  progression, hiring profile — sit above or outside his authority.
- Attribution is a fair thing to raise **once**, with evidence, in a calm
  setting. Then let it go and deliver.

## Adjustments context

Nick has disclosed ADHD/neurodivergence. An occupational health referral was
arranged by HR and the report has been received (`C:\TOM\Nick Ward -
Occupational Health Report.pdf`). The PIP states that if OH recommends
reasonable adjustments, the plan's actions and timescales **will be formally
reviewed and adjusted within 10 working days of receipt**.

This matters to the design of this workspace: a system that externalises
structure — that runs the proactive scan, holds the cadence and produces the
paper trail — is not a workaround. It is the reasonable adjustment, and it is
also the direct answer to "what happens at day 90". Build it that way and say so
plainly.
